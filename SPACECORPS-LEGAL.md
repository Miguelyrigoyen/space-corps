## OVERVIEW

Space Corps operates at the intersection of four distinct legal domains:

1. **Space law** — international treaties and domestic launch regulations
2. **Cryonics / human remains law** — state-by-state funeral and disposition regulations
3. **Consumer protection law** — contracts for future services
4. **Corporate liability** — business structure and risk containment

Each domain has real teeth. Understanding all four is essential before taking a single dollar from a customer for a preservation mission.

## PART 1: SPACE LAW

### The Outer Space Treaty (1967)
The foundational document governing all human activity in space. Signed by 114 nations including the US.

**Key provisions relevant to Space Corps:**

| Article | Provision | Implication |
|---|---|---|
| Article I | Space is the "province of all mankind" | Cannot claim ownership of orbital territory |
| Article II | No national appropriation of space or celestial bodies | No country owns the orbit your spacecraft is in |
| Article VI | Nations are responsible for activities of their nationals in space | The US government is legally responsible for Space Corps launches |
| Article VII | Launching state is liable for damage caused by space objects | Space Corps / its launch provider carries liability for debris or collision damage |
| Article VIII | Jurisdiction over objects in space remains with the launching state | US law governs your spacecraft even in orbit |
| Article IX | Avoid harmful contamination of space | Biohazard protocols for human remains payload |

**Bottom line:** Space is jurisdiction-free in terms of no nation owning it, but the US government retains legal authority over US-launched spacecraft and is internationally liable for them. You operate under US law in space.

---

### The Registration Convention (1976)
Requires all space objects to be registered with the UN Office for Outer Space Affairs (UNOOSA) and the launching state.

**What this means for Space Corps:**
- Every spacecraft must be registered with the FAA and reported to the UN
- The spacecraft receives an international designation
- This is actually a feature, not a burden — it gives the mission official legal standing

---

### The Liability Convention (1972)
Establishes that launching states are absolutely liable for damage caused by their space objects on Earth's surface and liable for damage in space if at fault.

**What this means for Space Corps:**
- Launch provider (SpaceX, etc.) carries primary liability insurance
- Space Corps should carry supplemental mission liability coverage
- Indemnification clauses in launch contracts are standard and essential

---

### FAA Launch Licensing
**The Commercial Space Launch Act** (and its amendments) gives the FAA authority over all US commercial space launches.

To launch, Space Corps needs:
- **FAA Mission License** or approval under a rideshare operator's license
- **Payload review** — FAA must approve the payload (human remains + cryogenic systems)
- **Safety approval** for cryogenic pressure systems aboard launch vehicle
- **Launch site operator coordination** (SpaceX handles much of this for Transporter rideshares)

**Payload-specific issues:**
- Cryogenic systems (LN2, pressurized vessels) require pressure vessel certification
- Human biological material is subject to additional FAA payload review
- No explicit prohibition on human remains in space — Celestis has done it repeatedly since 1997

**Precedent:** Celestis has received FAA payload approval for human cremated remains on multiple missions. Space Corps would be the first with vitrified biological material, which will require a more detailed payload safety review but is not categorically prohibited.

## PART 2: HUMAN REMAINS AND CRYONICS LAW

### Federal Level — No Specific Federal Cryonics Law
There is no federal statute specifically regulating cryopreservation. The FDA has not claimed jurisdiction over the process (it's not a medical treatment — the person is legally dead). The FTC has general consumer protection authority over the contracts.

### State-Level Regulation

Cryonics law is almost entirely state-level and highly fragmented.

**States with explicit cryonics provisions:**
- **Arizona** — Alcor's home state. Has the most developed legal framework for cryonics. Recognizes cryopreservation as a legal method of disposition alongside burial and cremation.
- **Michigan** — Has explicit statutory language recognizing cryonics arrangements.

**Most states:** Fall into a legal gray zone. Cryonics is not explicitly authorized or prohibited. It typically operates under:
- Funeral home laws (must involve a licensed funeral director)
- Body disposition statutes (next of kin has legal authority over disposition)
- Anatomical gift laws (can be used to authorize cryopreservation)

**Key risk:** If Space Corps operates as a cryonics provider (handling remains pre-launch), it may need funeral home licensing depending on the state where the member dies. The most common workaround is to partner with a licensed provider (like Alcor) to handle the preservation, then Space Corps handles launch and orbital storage.

### The Two-Phase Business Model (Legally Cleaner)

**Phase 1 — Preservation** (handled by licensed partner like Alcor or similar)
- Legal death declaration
- Stabilization and perfusion
- Vitrification to cryogenic temperature
- Transfer to Space Corps-designed transport dewar

**Phase 2 — Launch and Orbital Storage** (Space Corps)
- Payload integration
- Launch coordination
- Orbital operations
- Telemetry and memorial services

This division of responsibility:
- Keeps Space Corps out of the funeral services regulatory domain
- Lets a licensed cryonics provider handle the legally complex disposition phase
- Gives Space Corps a clean B2B relationship rather than direct handling of remains

### Human Remains in Space — Legal Precedent

| Mission | Company | Year | What Was Sent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pegasus (Gene Roddenberry) | Celestis | 1997 | Cremated remains |
| Lunar Prospector | Celestis | 1998 | Gene Shoemaker's ashes |
| SpaceLoft | UP Aerospace | 2006 | Various cremated remains |
| Elysium Star missions | Elysium Space | 2018–present | Cremated remains in orbit |

**Legal status of these missions:** All required FAA payload approval and coordination with state funeral authorities. No mission has yet sent biologically intact preserved remains — Space Corps would be the first.

This means there is no case law blocking it, but also no established approval pathway. Space Corps would be working with the FAA to create that pathway.

## PART 3: CONTRACTS AND CONSUMER PROTECTION

### The Core Challenge — Future Services
Space Corps is selling a service that won't be delivered for potentially years or decades. This creates significant consumer protection exposure.

**FTC Act Section 5** prohibits unfair or deceptive acts in commerce. Key requirements:
- No promises that cannot be kept
- No misleading claims about scientific certainty of revival
- Clear disclosure of risks
- Clear refund and cancellation policies

### What the Contract Must Cover

**1. Nature of the Service**
Explicit statement that Space Corps provides:
- Memorial orbital preservation services
- NOT a medical treatment
- NOT a guarantee of revival or resuscitation
- NOT a life insurance product

**2. Scientific Disclosure**
The contract should include a plain-language disclosure that:
- Cryopreservation is not proven to enable revival with current technology
- Revival may never be possible
- The mission is memorial in nature with the possibility of future scientific value
- No representation is made about the timeline or possibility of revival

**3. Payment Structure**
Options:
- **Full payment upfront** — simple but creates trust/escrow obligations
- **Life insurance-funded** — member pays premiums, policy pays Space Corps at time of death (Alcor's model — very strong legally)
- **Deposit + balance** — deposit secures the spot, balance due at time of service

**Recommendation:** Life insurance-funded model mirrors Alcor's legally vetted approach. Member names Space Corps as beneficiary of a specific policy. No money changes hands until service is delivered.

**4. Cancellation and Refund Policy**
- Clear window for cancellation with full refund (recommend 30 days from signing)
- Policy for what happens if Space Corps ceases operations before launch
- Trust account or escrow for funds held pending launch

**5. Assignment of Rights**
If a member dies and the family disputes the preservation wishes, who wins? The contract must:
- Be executed while the member is living and competent
- Reference the member's legal right to direct disposition of their own remains
- Include next-of-kin acknowledgment (strongly recommended)
- Integrate with the member's estate planning documents

**6. Force Majeure**
What happens if:
- The launch vehicle fails?
- Space Corps goes bankrupt?
- The cryonics partner fails?
- A government prohibits the mission?

The contract must address each scenario with clear refund or alternative service provisions.

---

### Regulatory Filings

Depending on how the funding model is structured:

| Structure | Regulator | Requirement |
|---|---|---|
| Selling "memberships" | FTC | Consumer protection compliance |
| Selling "prepaid funeral plans" | State insurance / funeral boards | May trigger prepaid funeral licensing |
| Life insurance beneficiary model | State insurance departments | No direct license needed — member's insurer is regulated |
| Investment/trust model | SEC (possibly) | If structured as investment, securities laws may apply |

**Safest structure:** Life insurance beneficiary model. Space Corps receives no money until service is rendered. No prepaid funeral licensing triggered. No securities issues.

## PART 4: INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY

### What to Protect

| Asset | Protection Method |
|---|---|
| "Space Corps" brand name | Trademark (USPTO) — file now |
| Spacecraft design | Design patent (if novel physical design) |
| Thermal management system | Utility patent (if novel process) |
| Software / control algorithms | Copyright (automatic) + trade secret |
| Customer data | Privacy policy + data security compliance |

### Trademark
File "SPACE CORPS" with the USPTO in relevant classes:
- **Class 40** — treatment of materials (preservation services)
- **Class 39** — transportation and storage
- **Class 44** — medical/funeral services (if applicable)

Note: "Space Corps" may conflict with existing military designations (US Space Corps was proposed legislation). A trademark attorney should run a clearance search before significant investment in the brand.

## PART 5: CORPORATE STRUCTURE

### Recommended Structure

```
SPACE CORPS HOLDINGS LLC (Parent)
│
├── Space Corps Operations LLC (Operating entity)
│   ├── Launch coordination
│   ├── Spacecraft operations
│   └── Customer contracts
│
└── Space Corps Memorial Trust (Customer funds)
    └── Holds prepaid funds in escrow until service delivery
```

**Why this structure:**
- Holdings LLC provides liability shield between operating risk and ownership
- Operations LLC is the entity that signs launch contracts, employment agreements, vendor agreements
- Separate trust/escrow for customer funds protects members if Operations LLC faces financial difficulty
- Clean separation makes fundraising and investor relations simpler

### Entity Type
**Delaware LLC or C-Corp** if planning to raise venture funding.
- C-Corp is required for VC investment
- LLC is simpler for bootstrapped or angel-funded phase

### Insurance Requirements
| Policy | Purpose |
|---|---|
| General Liability | Standard business liability |
| Launch Liability | Required by FAA and launch providers |
| Professional Liability (E&O) | Errors in service delivery |
| Directors & Officers | Protects leadership from personal liability |
| Cyber Liability | Customer data protection |

## PART 6: BIOETHICS AND REGULATORY RISK

### Bioethics Considerations
Regulatory bodies and the public may raise concerns about:

**1. Human Dignity**
Some religious and cultural groups object to cryopreservation on grounds that it disrespects human remains. Space Corps' memorial framing ("honoring" rather than "storing") significantly mitigates this perception risk.

**2. Exploitation of the Grieving**
Consumer protection regulators are sensitive to any service sold to people in emotional distress. All marketing must be clear, non-predatory, and non-exploitative. Contracts should be signed while the member is healthy, not by grieving families at time of death.

**3. Scientific Misrepresentation**
Overstating the probability of revival is the single biggest regulatory and reputational risk. Every piece of marketing should distinguish between:
- What is currently possible (preservation of biological structure)
- What is theoretically possible in the future (revival — not guaranteed)
- What Space Corps provides (memorial orbital preservation)

---

### Future Regulatory Risk
The regulatory landscape for space burials and cryonics is immature and actively evolving.

**Risks to watch:**
- FAA could create new payload categories for biological material in space
- States could update funeral home licensing laws to capture cryonics providers
- International pressure could emerge around "contamination" of space with biological material
- Public backlash could trigger legislative response

**Mitigation:** Early proactive engagement with the FAA, state regulators, and industry groups (Commercial Spaceflight Federation, ICCFA for funeral industry) positions Space Corps as a cooperative actor rather than a regulatory target.
